Privacy policy

Last updated 25 November 2026

Kaki is a meeting assistant. This page explains what Kaki stores, who processes it, how long it is kept, and how to delete it.

Signing in

Kaki uses Google sign-in only. When you sign in, Google confirms who you are, and Kaki receives your name, email address and profile photo. Kaki never sees your Google password and asks Google for nothing else.

Your first sign-in creates your account and a personal workspace. Kaki keeps its own account and session records, and a session cookie keeps you signed in for 12 hours.

A guest joins a meeting from its link without an account, using only the name they type. A guest's session is tied to that one meeting.

What Kaki stores

Everything below belongs to your account or to the workspace a meeting was held in.

  • Your account: your name, email address, profile photo, Google account id and sign-in session records.
  • Workspaces: the workspace name, its people and their roles, its invite link, and the meetings held in it.
  • Meetings: the title, agenda and timings; the live transcript, which is each person's spoken words with the speaker and time and which appears in the minutes; the open questions Kaki finds; its notes; and every to-do and draft. When a meeting ends, the minutes and to-dos stay in the workspace history.
  • Off the record: while it is on, Kaki does not transcribe, analyse or store the room's words. The minutes show only when it was on.
  • The search index: Kaki reads records from the tools a workspace connects (Gitea, Mattermost, the mail inbox and Wiki.js) and its own company records, plus the minutes of ended meetings. It splits them into chunks and stores the chunks with embeddings in its database so the Wingman can search them.
  • Tool logins: a workspace's tool logins are encrypted in the database and are never sent back to the browser. Anyone in the workspace sees only the non-secret settings.
  • Meeting audio and video: in normal use, Kaki does not record or keep them. Live audio and video are streamed through the meeting server, and each person's speech is sent to Azure Speech to be turned into text. Only the text is kept.

Who processes it

Kaki does not sell your data, show ads, or run analytics or advertising trackers. These are the outside services it uses.

  • Microsoft Azure (Southeast Asia) hosts Kaki, its Postgres database and the meeting audio and video server, and provides Azure Speech, which turns speech into text and produces the AI voices. Azure therefore handles stored meeting data and live meeting audio.
  • The NUS School of Computing AI gateway receives meeting text (transcripts, notes, questions, to-dos and drafts), the text of company records and the questions you ask, so it can write notes, answer questions and make search embeddings.
  • DeepSeek receives the same written requests only when the NUS gateway fails and Kaki falls back to it.
  • Google handles sign-in. It also serves your profile photo and the fonts the page uses, so it sees the usual request details, such as your IP address.
  • Your team's tools. Approved issues, posts, emails, wiki pages and calendar events go to the tools your workspace connects. In the default setup these run on the same server as Kaki. If an admin points a connection at another provider, that provider receives what Kaki writes there.

How long Kaki keeps it

Meetings, minutes and to-dos stay in their workspace until that workspace is deleted. A meeting you scheduled but have not started can be cancelled, which deletes it. Deleting a workspace removes its meetings, minutes, to-dos, tool logins and search-index entries straight away.

Deleting your account ends your open meetings, then removes the workspaces where you are the last member, their meetings and everything in them, your practice meetings, your memberships, your sessions and your account in one database transaction. A team workspace with other members stays, and meetings you opened there stay with the workspace. There is no waiting period in the app. A nightly backup can still hold a copy for up to 7 days, and log lines for up to 30 days.

A signed-out practice meeting is not kept: it exists only in the server's memory and goes when the room is unloaded or the server restarts. A signed-in person's practice meeting is kept in their own workspace history until they delete it.

An action Kaki has already written to your team's tools (a Gitea issue, a Mattermost post, an email, a wiki page or a calendar event) stays in that tool. Deleting your account does not remove it.

Logs and backups

The server keeps logs so the team can investigate problems. Those logs can contain meeting text, such as recognised speech, Kaki's replies, notes and task titles. Kaki keeps logs for up to 30 days and then removes them.

Kaki also backs up its database every night. Each backup is a compressed copy that only the machine's administrator can read, and Kaki keeps the newest 7 and removes older ones. A backup can therefore hold deleted meeting text for up to 7 days.

Deleting a meeting, workspace or account removes it from the app at once. Copies can stay in logs for up to 30 days and in backups for up to 7 days before they are removed.

Your choices

  • Anyone in a meeting can turn on Off the record.
  • A workspace admin can disconnect a tool, which deletes its stored login and the records Kaki read from it.
  • You can delete your account in the account menu by typing DELETE to confirm.
  • Guests need no account and keep none.
