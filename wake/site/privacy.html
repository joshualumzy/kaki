Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026

Kaki is a meeting assistant built as a student project for the IT5007 course at the National University of Singapore. This page says what Kaki keeps about you and who it shares it with.

What Kaki keeps

  • Your Google account basics when you sign in with Google: your name, email address and profile photo. Kaki asks Google for nothing else, and never sees your Google password.
  • Your workspace: every account gets its own workspace as soon as you sign in. You can make more workspaces and add people to them.
  • Your meetings: what was said (the transcript), the notes, decisions and to-dos Kaki writes, and the meeting minutes. These belong to the workspace the meeting was held in.
  • Connected tools: a workspace admin can connect Gitea, Mattermost, Mailpit email, Wiki.js or a calendar. Kaki stores the tool's login token encrypted, and reads records from the tool into a searchable index so it can answer questions in meetings.

Guests who join a meeting by link without signing in give only the name they type, and keep no account.

Who processes it

  • Microsoft Azure (Southeast Asia) hosts Kaki and its database, and turns meeting speech into text.
  • AI model providers read meeting text to write notes and answers: the NUS School of Computing AI gateway, and DeepSeek.
  • Google handles sign-in.

Kaki does not sell your data or show ads.

What you control

  • Everyone in a meeting sees a notice that it is transcribed before they join.
  • "Off the record" stops transcription until it is turned back on.
  • Disconnecting a tool deletes its stored login and the records Kaki read from it.
  • You can delete your account yourself in the account menu. Type DELETE to confirm. Your open meetings end, and your workspaces, meetings, minutes, to-dos, tool logins and sign-in sessions are removed. Other people keep their own accounts, but a team workspace you owned goes away with you.
  • For anything else, email the contact below.
